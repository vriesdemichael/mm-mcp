    Tools¶

    Everything your agent can do in Mattermost with mm-mcp. Ask in your own words; it picks the tool. Writing is off until you allow it.

    48 tools 26 only read 14 checked writes 7 yours alone 1 saves to your disk 5 show you a view

    • Catch up

      Pick up where you left off, in every channel, without marking anything read.

      “What did I miss since Friday?”

      read_unread get_user_channels list_mentions list_threads

    • Find it again

      Messages, files, people and channels, searched the way you would say it: by words, by who, where and when.

      “What did we decide about the release date?”

      search_posts search_files search_users search_channels

    • Read the whole story

      A channel page by page, a thread from its first post, and what was pinned or saved.

      “Summarise the thread about last night's outage.”

      read_channel read_post list_pinned_posts list_saved

    • See it as Mattermost shows it

      In an AI app that shows MCP Apps views, a thread, or what is waiting for you, appears in the chat as Mattermost lays it out: names and pictures, Markdown, reactions and images, and what is unread where.

      “Show me the thread about last night's outage.”

      show view_action view_change

    • Open the attachments

      Word, Excel and PowerPoint as their text, PDFs, images and archives, read for the model, or saved to your disk.

      “What's in the spreadsheet Sam shared?”

      read_file save_file

    • Post, reply and react

      Messages, replies, direct and group messages, edits and reactions, each approved by your AI app and checked by mm-mcp before it goes out.

      “Tell Sam in the thread that the notes are ready.”

      create_post dm group_message update_post delete_post add_reaction remove_reaction pin_post

    • Channels and people

      Who is who and who is around, which channels exist and who is in them; join, leave, create, add people, and set your status.

      “Is Sam online, and who else is in ~release-planning?”

      get_me get_users get_status get_user_teams get_team_info get_channel_info list_team_channels list_archived_channels get_channel_stats join_channel leave_channel add_channel_members create_channel set_status

    • Your own things

      A post proposed as your draft, to send yourself, reminders, saved posts and followed threads. Nobody else sees them; deleting a draft, which loses your words, is checked.

      “Draft a reply I can send myself, and remind me at nine tomorrow.”

      propose_post list_drafts delete_draft set_post_reminder save_post follow_thread mark_channel_read typing

    • Check the setup

      When something fails: the connection, the login, and who decides on a write.

      “Why can't you post?”

      diagnose

    Every tool¶

    Grouped as above. A badge says when a tool does more than read: checked for a change others see, or words of yours it deletes, which the call states and mm-mcp checks (below); yours alone for what only you see or is gone in seconds; and your disk. A tool marked view also shows you what it did in the chat, in an AI app that shows views; the badge opens it, live, on Views. The tools that write are offered only once you allow writes, with MM_MCP_ALLOW_WRITES (Configuration).

    Catch up¶

    read_unread: Read unread posts
    Catches up on a channel from where you stopped reading: a few posts you have read, then the ones you have not, oldest first, with the first unread one named. A channel you never opened is all unread, and reads newest first, the pages after going further back. It marks nothing read: what you have read is yours to record.
    get_user_channels: List the user's channels
    The channels you belong to, direct and group messages included, most recently active first, each with its team and how many messages and mentions are unread. team_id keeps one team's, unread_only those with something unread.
    list_mentions: List mentions
    The posts that mention you, most recent first, as Mattermost's Recent Mentions finds them: by @username, and by your first name, the other words and @channel, @all and @here when your notification settings say those mention you. Only a post that mentions you as Mattermost would notify you is kept, and none of your own. The answer names the words it looked for. from, in, before, after and on narrow it, and like search_posts it reaches the 100 most recent matches at most. The database search Team Edition uses skips "all" and "here" as too common to index, so @all and @here are found only on a server that searches with Elasticsearch, as they are in Mattermost's own Recent Mentions.
    list_threads: List threads
    The threads you follow, as Mattermost's threads view lists them, most recently replied to first: the post that started each, who took part, and how many replies and mentions you have not read.

    Find it again¶

    search_posts: Search posts
    Messages you can read, across every team or one, by words, and by who wrote them, where and when through from, in, before, after and on, so no search syntax is needed. @username finds where someone was mentioned. 20 posts a page by default, at most 100. Mattermost's search finds the 100 most recent matches at most, and the answer says capped when it reached them: before reaches older ones. in takes a channel you belong to, the only ones Mattermost searches, and from a username, refused with the closest when nobody has it. The days before, after and on name are your own, in the timezone you set in Mattermost, as in its search box.
    search_files: Search files
    Files attached to posts you can read, by name, by type with ext:pdf, and by from, in, before, after and on, each with the post and channel it is in and their links, and the file's address as a resource. Like search_posts, it finds the 100 most recent matches at most.
    search_users: Search users
    Users whose username, name, nickname or email address contains a term, optionally only within a team or a channel. Deactivated users are left out. Mattermost finds at most 1000 for one term.
    search_channels: Search channels
    Channels by part of their name: your own, and public ones you have not joined. Mattermost finds at most 50 public channels in a team for one term.

    Read the whole story¶

    read_channel: Read channel

    A channel's messages, 30 a page by default and at most 200, oldest first within a page: the newest, and the pages after go further back. before starts back from a post; after and since, from a post or a time, read forward. since takes a time with an offset, or a time or a day in your own timezone. collapse_threads leaves the replies out and shows each thread by the post that started it, as Mattermost shows a channel with collapsed reply threads.

    A page here or of read_unread can hold a few more posts than its limit: posts written in the same millisecond stay on one page, since Mattermost reads on from a post by its time alone, and a page split inside a millisecond would lose posts.

    read_post: Read post
    A post and the thread it is in, oldest first, 100 posts a page by default. Give any post in the thread; include_thread false reads the post alone.
    list_pinned_posts: List pinned posts
    The posts pinned to a channel.
    list_saved: List saved posts
    The posts you saved to come back to.

    See it as Mattermost shows it¶

    show: Show view

    Puts what you asked to see in front of you in the chat, laid out as Mattermost lays it out.

    • Kind thread shows a thread: who wrote each post, with their profile picture, when, the Markdown drawn, @mentions and ~channels, reactions with who reacted, images you can open whole and file cards, and the edited and pinned marks. It shows the first 30 posts, says how many more the thread has, and loads more as you ask.
    • Kind unreads shows what is waiting for you: up to 20 channels with something unread, grouped by team, with their unread and mention counts; up to 10 posts that mention you; and up to 10 threads you follow with unread replies. It says when Mattermost has more. A channel's unread messages or a thread open in place, or in Mattermost where your app does not let the view load them, and once you allow writes, a channel can be marked read from the view.

    Links open in Mattermost. Your agent calls it when you ask to see something, after it found it, or ask what is waiting for you. It answers with what read_post, or get_user_channels, list_mentions and list_threads, answer, so the chat holds nothing the view adds. In an app that shows no views it shows nothing, and says so, and your agent answers in words (Views).

    view_action: Act in a view
    What you do in a view that changes nothing, such as loading more of a thread or opening a channel's unread messages in place, goes through this tool. Only a view calls it: an app that shows views keeps it from your agent, and mm-mcp offers it only to an app that says it shows views.
    view_change: Change in a view
    What you do in a view that changes something goes through this tool: marking a channel read from the unread view, as mark_channel_read does, and posting, changing or discarding a proposal (propose_post). Only a view calls it, on your press: an app that shows views keeps it from your agent, and mm-mcp offers it only to an app that says it shows views, once you allow writes.

    Open the attachments¶

    read_file: Read file
    A file attached to a post, as content the model can read: text in windows of numbered lines, chosen with start_line and line_count; Word, PowerPoint and Excel files as their text; zip and tar archives as a listing; images as images, turned upright and scaled down when large; small audio, video and PDF files as themselves, for a client that can read them. Anything else is described by its type and size, with the post's link. Files over 64 MiB are described without being read.
    save_file: Save file
    Saves a file attached to a post into your download directory, set with MM_MCP_DOWNLOAD_DIR and your Downloads directory by default, under its own name, and answers with the path. An existing file is never overwritten: a number is added to the name instead, and saving the same file again answers with the copy already saved. The file is marked as downloaded from the internet, as a browser marks it: on Windows, SmartScreen checks a program and Office opens a document in Protected View; on macOS, Gatekeeper checks it. Files up to 100 MiB. Offered only when mm-mcp runs on your machine, whether writes are allowed or not (below).

    Post, reply and react¶

    Your MCP client's approval of each call decides, and mm-mcp checks what each states before it acts (below).

    create_post: Create post view

    Posts a message in a channel, or with root_id replies in the thread of any post in it. Where mm-mcp asks, its question shows the message as it will be sent, the channel and, for a reply, the start of the thread. Posting takes down the typing indicator typing put up there. Returns the post, which an app that shows MCP Apps views shows in the chat as posted, under where it went, as dm and group_message do (Views).

    Every call states how far the message goes (below): reaches, how many members the channel has, and notifies where the message alerts a whole channel or user group as Mattermost would ask about first.

    files attaches up to ten files: text the model writes, given a name and content, or, when the server runs on your machine over stdio, a file by its full path. Where mm-mcp asks, its question lists each file with its size, type and the path it was read from, the box you tick names every file read from your disk by its path, the files are uploaded only once you accept, and a file changed after you were asked is refused. A file where credentials are kept is never attached (Security); attach such a file in Mattermost yourself if you mean to. dm and group_message take files too.

    dm: Send direct message view
    Sends a direct message to one person, by username or email address, or a note to yourself by your own username. The username is required, so whatever approves the call sees who it goes to. This is how to message people: "message Alice and Bob" is two direct messages.
    group_message: Send group message view
    Sends one message to two to seven people together, in the group conversation Mattermost keeps for exactly them. Only when you ask for a group conversation.
    update_post: Update post
    Replaces the text of one of your own posts. Where mm-mcp asks, its question shows the current text and its replacement. An edit notifies nobody, so its mentions are not checked, only its length. The call states reaches, how many members the post's channel has, since an edit reaches everyone the post does; old_message, the post's current text in full; and, for a post with reactions or replies, responses, their number (below). Another person's post is refused, even with an administrator's credential.
    delete_post: Delete post
    Deletes one of your own posts. Deleting the post that starts a thread deletes its replies, other people's included. The call states deletes, how many posts go: 1 for a reply, or the post and its replies (below).
    add_reaction: Add reaction
    Reacts to a post with an emoji, by name, such as thumbsup, or as itself, such as 👍. An unknown name is refused with the closest ones. A reaction joins one others gave under another name of the same emoji, so thumbsup beside a +1 is the one 👍. Reacting again with the same emoji changes nothing.
    remove_reaction: Remove reaction
    Takes back one of your reactions.
    pin_post: Pin post
    Pins a post to its channel for everyone, or unpins it with pinned false.

    Channels and people¶

    get_me: Who am I
    The Mattermost user the server acts as: id, username, name, nickname, position, roles, the timezone set in Mattermost, and whether it is a bot. Call it to check the connection and whose access the other tools use.
    get_users: Get users
    People by username, user id or email address, any mix of them in one call, with whether each is a bot or deactivated. An email address finds someone only when the server shows you addresses. A name nobody has is listed with the closest usernames beside the people found, and refused only when nobody is found.
    get_status: Get status
    Whether people are around: online, away, do not disturb or offline, when they were last active, and the status message they set.
    get_user_teams: List the user's teams
    The teams you belong to.
    get_team_info: Get team
    A team by id, name or link. An open team you are not in is found by the exact name in its link.
    get_channel_info: Get channel
    A channel by id, name or link, among your own channels and the public channels of your teams, saying whether you belong to it and whether it is archived.
    list_team_channels: List a team's channels
    A team's public channels, by name.
    list_archived_channels: List archived channels
    A team's archived channels. An archived channel can be read but not posted in.
    get_channel_stats: Get channel stats
    How many people belong to a channel, how many are guests, and how many posts are pinned and files shared in it.
    join_channel: Join channel
    Joins a public channel of one of your teams. Everyone in it sees that you joined. A private channel is joined only by being added.
    leave_channel: Leave channel
    Leaves a channel you belong to. Everyone in it sees that you left, and only being added brings you back to a private one, which mm-mcp's question says where it asks.
    add_channel_members: Add channel members
    Adds up to twenty people, by username, user id or email address, to a channel you belong to. Each is notified, and where mm-mcp asks, its question names each.
    create_channel: Create channel
    Creates a public or private channel in one of your teams, with you in it, the name in its link made from its display name as Mattermost's own app makes it, and its purpose and header when given.
    set_status: Set status
    Sets your status as everyone sees it beside your name: online, away, do not disturb until a time, or offline, and a status message with an emoji, until a time or until changed; or clears the message. A time without an offset is read in your timezone.

    Your own things¶

    propose_post: Propose post view

    Proposes a post for you to send yourself. The message waits as your draft, in a channel, a thread, or a direct or group message you already have, in your message box in every Mattermost app, and the answer links where it waits. Nothing is sent. The message is checked as a post's is, and its notes say what its mentions will do. One that starts with / is refused, since Mattermost runs it as a slash command when you send it. Proposing again replaces mm-mcp's own proposal while you have not changed it; a draft you wrote or typed in is never replaced (below). A conversation that does not exist yet is not started, since starting one shows it to the other people at once, and a proposal is refused when your drafts do not sync, since you would never see it. What you send from Mattermost goes out as yours, without the mark of a post written with AI. Whatever your app approves, it is a way to post only what you send yourself.

    In an app that shows views, the proposal appears in the chat as it will be posted, where it waits, and what its mentions do, with Post, Edit and Discard (Views). Each acts on the text the view shows, and only while your draft still holds it as the view put it there: a draft you typed on in Mattermost is yours to send from there, and a newer proposal is drawn again as it is now. Post marks the post as written with AI, and asks first, in Mattermost's own words, where Mattermost would ask before an @channel or @here reaches more than five people. A link shows where it leads, a character you cannot see or that turns the text's direction is flagged, and the text as written is a click away. Where a draft of yours is in the way, the view shows yours beside the proposal, or the proposal as a revision of yours when your agent says so with revises_draft, and nothing is written until you press Replace mine, or Apply, or Keep mine. MM_MCP_POST_FROM_VIEWS=false takes Post out of the view (Configuration).

    list_drafts: List drafts
    Your drafts, in channels and threads, with where each is, by link and address, and its length, which delete_draft states.
    delete_draft: Delete draft
    Deletes your draft in a channel or thread. Nobody else sees a draft, but it may hold words you have not sent, so the call states discards, how many characters the draft holds, as list_drafts gives its length (below), and where mm-mcp asks, its question shows the draft and where it is.
    set_post_reminder: Set post reminder
    Has Mattermost remind you of a post at a time, as its "Remind me" does. A time without an offset is read in the timezone you set in Mattermost.
    save_post: Save post
    Saves a post among your saved posts, or removes it with saved false.
    follow_thread: Follow thread
    Follows a thread, so its replies notify you and it appears in list_threads, or stops following it with following false.
    mark_channel_read: Mark channel read
    Marks a channel read, as opening it in Mattermost does, clearing its unread count and mentions, only when you ask for it. Reading a channel for you never marks it read.
    typing: Show typing
    Shows you typing in a channel or thread while a message is written, kept up until create_post posts there, stop is sent, or a minute passes. It is optional, for a long message that takes a while to write.

    Check the setup¶

    diagnose: Diagnose mm-mcp
    The checks mm-mcp doctor makes, from inside the running server, so with the configuration your MCP client actually started it with: the address, which credential it acts with, a login mm-mcp login stored and whether MM_TOKEN hides it, the proxy, whether the server answers as a supported Mattermost, whose credential it is and whether that user belongs to a team. It adds what only the client shows: its name and protocol version, who decides on a write, and, where mm-mcp is set to ask, whether the client can show its question. With ask_test_question, it shows you a test question and reports how it was answered: an answer within a few seconds came from the client itself, which then answers every question before a write without showing it. Each check says what it found, and a failed one what to do. It never returns the credential (Configuration).

    Good to know¶

    Works the same everywhere¶

    • Names and links work where ids do, for a tool that reads. Every channel_id and team_id of a tool that reads, or that changes only what is yours, takes a name as well as an id, a channel's with ~ or # before it or not. Every id argument takes the place's address, and the link Mattermost shows you: a channel's, a direct or group message's, a team's, a post's, Copy Link's or the one a thread opens at, and a file's, or its resource address. A name of a channel, a team or a person matches in any case, whole or in part; an ambiguous one is refused with every candidate, and an unknown one with the closest names. A link is never fetched, and one to another server is refused (ADR-030).
    • A write others see takes an address. The tools that post or change what others see take a channel, a post or a team by its address alone, matched exactly: mattermost://<server>/<team>/channels/<channel>, a direct message's mattermost://<server>/<team>/messages/@<username>, a post's as its channel's address followed by its id, and a team's as mattermost://<server>/<team>. Your MCP client's approval of the call, or its AI reviewer, sees the tool's arguments and nothing else, so the address shows where the write goes by name. An id, a name or a link is refused with where to read the address, and so is the old address of a renamed channel.
    • Every place comes with its link and its address. Each post, channel, team and draft an answer describes carries its address, and the link it opens at, and so do the post and channel of each file search_files finds, made from names: a channel's in its team, a direct or group message's in your first team, as /<team>/messages/@<username>, and a post's in its channel's team, as Copy Link gives it. Asked where something was said, the agent links the post, not the conversation. Beside each link is the name Mattermost shows: a channel's display name, and a direct or group message named after the other people in it.
    • Every list pages the same way. Give limit, and pass the next_cursor an answer ends with back as cursor, with the same other arguments, for the next page, until an answer has none (ADR-032).
    • Every post comes back in one shape: its link and address, its author's username and name, its channel by name, link and address and its team by name, its files with their ids and resource addresses, reactions with who reacted, responses, how many reactions and replies it has, whether it is pinned or marked as written with AI, its thread, and when it was edited. An answer whose posts are all in one channel names the channel and team once.
    • A list cuts a long message short. A message longer than 4,000 characters is cut, with its whole length said; read_post reads it whole.
    • Times are your own. A time in an answer is in the timezone you set in Mattermost, with its offset, such as 2026-10-09T09:30:00+02:00, and a time you give without an offset is read in it too. With no timezone set, UTC.

    Files as resources¶

    Every file attached to a post is also an MCP resource, at mattermost://<your server>/files/<file id>, from a template mm-mcp lists (ADR-029). A client can read one when it needs it, and some let you attach one to the conversation or save it. It reads as read_file reads the file, but whole: text, a Word, PowerPoint or Excel file's text and an archive's listing as text, an image as the image read_file returns, and anything else as itself, in one message of up to 10 MiB, a binary file counted as its base64: MCP sends a resource whole, and clients drop a larger message. MM_MCP_RESOURCE_MAX_MB changes that for a client that takes more (Configuration). A file larger than your Mattermost now takes in one, its Maximum File Size, is not downloaded. Past a limit, a text is read with read_file, in windows, and a file saved with save_file. Each post's files, and each file search_files finds, give the address, the answer links each file as a resource, and every file_id takes the address. An image also gives its size and its preview's address, at mattermost://<your server>/files/<file id>/preview: the smaller image its post shows, cheaper to look at than the whole image.

    The pictures a post shows with it are resources too: a person's profile picture, at mattermost://<your server>/users/<user id>/image, the one they set or the one Mattermost draws from their initials, and a custom emoji's, at mattermost://<your server>/emoji/<name>. Each reads as a picture, PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP and never SVG, and one read again within a few minutes comes from what mm-mcp kept rather than from Mattermost. A client that completes resource addresses offers, as you type part of a file's name, the ids of the files that match. Threads and conversations are not resources: a conversation has no end to read it whole to.

    Views¶

    In an AI app that shows MCP Apps views, show puts a thread, or what is unread for you, in the chat as Mattermost lays it out, every post your agent makes shows itself there as posted, and a post it proposes with propose_post waits there for you to post, change or discard (ADR-022). The view is a page in mm-mcp itself, which reaches no network: the app reads what it shows from mm-mcp, and the pictures as resources, with mm-mcp's credential. Where the app reads no pictures, people are their initials and a custom emoji its name. The page takes the app's light or dark theme and its language, where mm-mcp has words for it, and English otherwise. Goose's desktop app shows views, and so does VS Code with its chat.mcp.apps.enabled setting on; diagnose says whether yours does. Views shows each of them, live.

    How a write is checked¶

    Your MCP client's own approval of each call decides whether a change others see goes ahead: its prompt, its permission rules or its auto mode (ADR-033). mm-mcp does not ask you by default. What it controls is what it checks itself, on every call, whoever approved it: how far a post or an edit goes, the text an edit replaces and the reactions and replies that answer it, and what a deletion takes away, each stated in the call and read again just before the write; where it goes, by address; and the message itself. A refusal says what mm-mcp found and tells the model to weigh whether that is what you meant.

    A post can also go ahead on your own press. In an app that shows views, a post your agent proposes with propose_post shows with Post, which sends it only while your draft holds what the view shows, after Mattermost's own question where it would ask one (Views). MM_MCP_POST_FROM_VIEWS=false takes Post out of the view.

    With MM_MCP_ELICITATION_OPT_IN=true, mm-mcp asks you as well (Configuration): it shows what will change, where, and under whose name, and acts only when you tick the box and accept. The question is an MCP elicitation, a form the client shows, so these tools then need a client that shows one; a client that declares it cannot gets an error that says so, and nothing is written. Some clients declare that they can and decline the form without showing it, as Claude Code does in the Claude desktop app's Code tab. diagnose with ask_test_question tells you whether yours shows it. MM_MCP_FORCE_HUMAN_IN_THE_LOOP_IN_CLAUDE_CODE has Claude Code ask on every call instead.

    Every message is checked before the write (ADR-031): one longer than the server takes is refused with its length and the limit, and an @mention of someone nobody is is refused with the closest usernames. Where mm-mcp asks, the question says how many people @here, @channel and @all reach, who of those mentioned is deactivated, and, on a licensed server with user groups, how many a group's mention notifies, and puts what a post does, and every file it carries, before the message. Mentions are found as Mattermost finds them, so code mentions nobody and @here. ending a sentence is @here.

    How far a message goes¶

    create_post states in its call how far the message goes, as two numbers mm-mcp counts itself (ADR-031):

    • reaches, on every post: how many members the channel has. Everyone in it can read the message, whatever it mentions.
    • notifies, exactly where Mattermost's web app would ask before sending, with the number it would show: everyone but you for @channel, @all or @here in a channel of more than 5 members, or a mentioned user group's members in the channel, above 5. As in Mattermost, it is not asked while the server's EnableConfirmNotificationsToChannel is off, or of someone who may not mention the whole channel.

    update_post states reaches too, never notifies. The numbers are in the call because that is what your MCP client's own approval shows you, or its AI reviewer, and the model cannot state them without looking. A missing or wrong number is refused with the real one, and the model is told to weigh whether you meant the message for that many people before it calls again. Each is counted again just before the post goes out, so members who joined since the count was read stop it.

    For example, when the model posts @channel the release is out in ~Town Square, a channel of 512 members, without stating the numbers, the call is refused with:

    Refused

    create_post did not run: a message in ~Town Square reaches 512 people, the channel's members as Mattermost counts them, and the call left the number out. Consider whether the person meant this message for all of them. If they did, call again with reaches: 512. If not, send it somewhere narrower, such as a smaller channel or a direct or group message, or ask them where it should go

    With reaches: 512 it is refused again, because Mattermost would ask before alerting the whole channel:

    Refused

    create_post did not run: @channel alerts 511 people, every member of ~Town Square but the user, which Mattermost asks about before sending, and the call left the number out. Consider whether the person meant to alert all of them. If they did, call again with notifies: 511. If not, mention only the people meant, post somewhere narrower, or ask them

    The model then calls again with reaches: 512 and notifies: 511, which your client's approval shows you, as does mm-mcp's question where it asks. Nothing is posted until both are right.

    What an edit changes¶

    update_post states the post's current text in full, old_message, alongside its replacement (ADR-031). The model cannot edit a post without writing out all of what it replaces, and your client's approval shows both texts. An old_message that differs from the post's current text, because you edited the post after the model read it or because only part of it is quoted, is refused with the current text.

    A post with reactions, or with replies in the thread it starts, also takes responses, their number, as read_post gives it. An edit leaves those reactions and replies under the replacing text, and Mattermost tells nobody about an edit, so a thumbs up given to one sentence stands under another. For example, an edit of your "Give me a thumbs up if you like cat memes", which has five 👍 and two replies, that leaves out responses is refused with:

    Refused

    update_post did not run: 7 reactions and replies respond to your post in ~Town Square: :thumbsup: ×5 (@brent, @ann, @joe, 2 more), 2 replies in its thread, and the call left the number out. An edit leaves them under the replacing text, and Mattermost tells nobody about it. Consider whether they fit the replacement; if the change is news to those who responded, reply in the thread instead. If they fit it, call again with responses: 7

    A post without reactions or replies takes no responses.

    What a write takes away¶

    A deletion states how much goes in its call, as a number mm-mcp reads itself: discards, the characters of the draft delete_draft deletes, as list_drafts gives each draft's length; and deletes, the posts delete_post removes, the post and every reply in its thread. A missing or wrong number is refused with the real one and the start of what would go, and the model is told to weigh whether you meant to lose it. Each is counted again just before the write, so a draft you typed on or a reply that arrived since the model read it stops the write.

    For example, deleting a post that started a thread of 12 replies, without deletes:

    Refused

    delete_post did not run: it deletes 13 posts, your post in ~Town Square, which begins “Who is in for the release party on Friday? I'll book a table”, and the 12 replies in its thread, other people's included, and the call left the number out. Consider whether the person meant to delete all of them. If they did, call again with deletes: 13. If not, leave it, or ask the person

    A thirteenth reply arriving before the model calls again with deletes: 13 gets the same refusal, with 14.

    propose_post takes nothing away. A draft of yours where the proposal would go, one you wrote or typed in, is left as it is. In an app that shows views, the view asks you which to keep; in one that does not, the call is refused with what the draft holds. For example, proposing a reply in ~Town Square, where you started one of your own:

    Refused

    propose_post did not run: the person has a draft there, which was left as it is: “Sounds good, but can we move it to”. If they asked you to change it, delete it with delete_draft, stating discards: 34, and propose again. If not, they can finish or clear it in Mattermost, or post with create_post

    When you asked for your draft to be changed, the model deletes it with delete_draft, whose call states its length, and proposes again.

    Where a write goes¶

    A write others see names its channel, post or team by its address (above, ADR-030), so your client's approval shows where it goes by name. A post to ~Town Square named by its channel's name, on a server at chat.example.com, is refused with:

    Refused

    channel_id: ~town-square is a name, but this takes an address, mattermost://chat.example.com/<team>/channels/<channel>, or <team>/messages/@<username> for a direct message, which names the place exactly and shows it to whoever approves the call; get_user_channels, get_channel_info and every post give each channel's address

    and a reply whose root_id names the post by its link:

    Refused

    root_id: https://chat.example.com/eng/pl/4xk3m9p7qfgt8jw1yb5hc2ndre is a link, but this takes an address, mattermost://chat.example.com/<team>/channels/<channel>/<post id>: its channel's address, then its id, which names the place exactly and shows it to whoever approves the call; read_post, read_channel and search_posts give each post's address

    The model reads the address, mattermost://chat.example.com/eng/channels/town-square, and calls again with it.

    Where mm-mcp asks, what a write acts on is what you were asked about: a reply posted, a file rewritten or a draft typed on while you read the question stops the write. Posts and edits are marked as written with AI, as Mattermost shows it, unless MM_MCP_MARK_AI_GENERATED is false. A proposal is not: what you send from Mattermost is yours.

    On your machine¶

    save_file, and attaching a file by its path, are offered only when mm-mcp runs on your own machine, started by your client over stdio, whether writes are allowed or not: they reach your disk, not Mattermost (ADR-029).

    Not offered¶

    • Scheduled posts. Mattermost's scheduled posts need a licence, and Team Edition, which mm-mcp is tested against, refuses them (ADR-004).
    • Marking what the model read as read, or anything unread. What you have read is yours to record, and a model reading a channel is not you reading it; mark_channel_read marks a channel read when you ask (ADR-021).

    How the tools are built¶

    Every tool declares a title and the four MCP annotations: whether it only reads, whether it may destroy something, whether repeating it changes nothing more, and that it works in a closed domain, your one Mattermost server, which the MCP specification counts as closed however freely people write in it (ADR-021). They are hints: a client may use them to decide what to ask you about, or ignore them, and none of them is a security control. Some clients run a tool marked read-only without asking you or their AI reviewer, so only a tool that changes nothing in Mattermost is marked so, and a test holds each one to the endpoints it calls.

    Each tool also accounts for every parameter of the Mattermost endpoints it calls: which argument sets it, or why it is fixed or left out (ADR-028), and a live test calls every tool against a real Mattermost (ADR-004).
